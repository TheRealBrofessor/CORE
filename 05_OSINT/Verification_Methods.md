# What It Is

A general framework for checking whether a claim is true before accepting or repeating it: identify the original source, corroborate with independent sources, check the date/context, and separate verified fact from interpretation.

# Why It Matters

This is the connective method underlying every OSINT note in this vault — [[Search_Operators]], [[Source_Ranking]], [[Domain_OSINT]], [[Username_OSINT]], and [[Image_OSINT]] are all tools in service of this one goal: not being fooled, and not fooling others.

# When To Use It

Before treating any claim — a news story, a domain's identity, an image's context, a technical assertion — as established fact.

# How To Use It Safely

1. Trace the claim to its earliest/original source.
2. Check whether independent sources corroborate it (not just re-shares of the same original).
3. Check the date and whether the context still applies.
4. Explicitly label what's verified fact, what's your interpretation, and what's still uncertain — this mirrors how every CORE note is expected to be written (see [[CORE_Principles]]).
5. Be willing to conclude "unverified" rather than force a conclusion.

# Common Mistakes

- Stopping at the first source that confirms what you already believed (confirmation bias).
- Treating volume of shares/reposts as evidence of accuracy.
- Presenting a verified fact and your own inference from it as if they carry equal certainty.
