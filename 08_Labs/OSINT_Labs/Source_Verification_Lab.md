# Objective

Practice applying [[Verification_Methods]] and [[Source_Ranking]] end-to-end on a real, already-public claim of your choosing.

# Safety Scope

Fully passive — reading and comparing publicly available sources. No contact with, or probing of, any private individual or system. Choose a public, low-stakes claim (a news story, a technical claim, a viral post) rather than anything involving a real person's private information.

# Requirements

- Internet access and a search engine.
- A specific claim to verify (pick one: a news headline, a "fact" you've seen shared, or a technical claim from a blog post).

# Steps

1. Write down the exact claim you're verifying, in one sentence.
2. Find the earliest/original source of the claim using [[Search_Operators]] (e.g. `"exact phrase"` search, or `site:` search if you suspect a specific origin).
3. Find at least one independent corroborating source — not just a re-share of the same original.
4. Check the date of the original claim and whether the context still applies today.
5. Apply [[Source_Ranking]]: classify each source you found as primary, secondary, or unverified.
6. Write a short conclusion: verified, partially verified, or unverified — and why.

# Expected Result

A short written verification report: the claim, sources checked, their ranking, and your conclusion, clearly separating verified fact from your own interpretation (per [[CORE_Principles]]).

# Troubleshooting

- If you can't find an original source, that itself is a finding — note it as "origin untraceable" rather than guessing.
- If sources conflict, don't average them into a vague middle conclusion — state the conflict explicitly.

# Cleanup

None required.
